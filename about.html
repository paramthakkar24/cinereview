    About CineReview

    We are a small team that watches films all day so that you do not have to gamble an evening on one. Here is what we do, how we arrive at a score, and how to get hold of us.

    Our mission

    To make the decision of what to watch obvious, honest and quick — without pretending that a film is better than it is.

    There is more to choose from on any given Friday than anyone has time to see. CineReview exists to close that gap. Every film in our library gets the same treatment: watched in full, written up at length, and given a single score out of ten that we are willing to defend.

    We do not take money for coverage, we do not run trailers as advertisements, and we do not publish a review for a film we have not watched. Where a movie falls short, we say so in the same review as where it succeeds — because a list of only the good things is not a review, it is a brochure.

    How we score

    A CineReview score is out of ten, and we do not use half stars. It reflects the film as a whole — writing, performances, direction, craft and the thing that is hardest to quantify, which is whether the film knows what it is doing.

    Alongside the score we publish the full reader breakdown, so you can see whether a 7.4 was loved by everyone who saw it or split the room down the middle. Our scores are also cross-referenced against a broader critics index, which is shown separately and never blended into our own number.

    The point of all this

    Why movie reviews are worth reading

    Reviews are not just a score attached to a title. They are a way of thinking about a film together.

    01

    Time is the real currency

    Two hours is a long way from an ordinary Tuesday. Knowing a film is a waste before you spend it is the single most useful thing a review can do for you.

    02

    A second opinion catches what you miss

    We watch for craft: how a cut lands, what a performance withholds, whether the third act earns its ending. That attention frees you to watch for what the film makes you feel.

    03

    Ratings find your people

    A breakdown tells you more than a total. A 7.5 that split the room is a very different proposition from a 7.5 that nobody argued about — and worth knowing before you press play.

    04

    Discoveries you would not have made

    The best reason to read reviews is that they surface films outside your habits — an animation you did not think of watching, a documentary that rewrites what you assumed about a subject.
